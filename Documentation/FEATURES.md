# Features

What Amgi does today. For how it is put together, see [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Studying

- **FSRS scheduling** — the official Rust FSRS engine, not a reimplementation. Ratings, intervals, and review history are computed by the same code as Anki Desktop.
- **Review session** — Again/Hard/Good/Easy with the next interval printed on every button, session progress (the bar, with the card count beside it), rating toast with auto-advance, native typed-answer field, tap-to-play audio, and undo (in ⋯, or ⌘Z). The top bar holds seven buttons, few enough that iOS never tucks any behind a "⋯" of its own: close, a pencil that opens the card's note for editing in one tap, hands-free, a 1_critical mark (a filled red triangle when the card's deck is 1_critical or one inside it, by the deck it came from when it's in a filtered deck; tapped, it moves the card there, making the deck first if there's none, or, when it's there, back to the deck it came from, or 1_Neuro_Life when that isn't known or is gone; the card stays on screen to be answered as usual, with its scheduling worked out again under its new deck's settings and the answer buttons' intervals to match, since Anki won't take an answer made with the old ones), the card's flag (filled in its colour when it has one; tap to choose another or take it off), the visual mnemonic, and ⋯ for everything else.
- **Time left** — under the progress bar, "About 12 min left · done around 3:42 PM", worked out from how fast you've answered so far this session and how many cards are left; when studying a deck, with an extra turn for each new card's learning step and for the repeats your misses bring back. Hide it from the ⋯ menu while reviewing, or Settings → Review → Progress.
- **Today's minimum: Reviews and New** — the Library's top card has one big button for reviews and one for new cards. Each shows what's left of a total that's fixed for the day, "120 of 300": Reviews counts every card that was due today and has no flag and isn't in deck "p", New counts today's new cards from every deck but "p", within each deck's daily limit. Each round shows every card once, shuffled across decks; a card you miss doesn't come back in the round, so the count only goes down. Leave part way and you pick up where you left off. Finishing a round shows "Done for today" (or which half is left) for a moment and goes back to the Library, and once both are done the top card turns green: today's minimum is met. After that, Reviews offers **Due again**, a round of the cards seen today that are due again, each once, as often as you like. The numbers come from Anki's own searches (answered today, first learned today), so they hold across restarts and other devices and reset with Anki's day. New cards learned this way are charged to their home decks' daily limits when the session closes, as studying the deck itself would.
- **End-of-day summary** — when the round that finishes today's minimum closes, a sheet shows time studied, how much you got right, and your 5 hardest cards: the ones you missed today that you've forgotten most often. They start ticked; one tap flags them red for the Graveyard. Reopen it any time that day from "Today's summary" on the green top card.
- **Today widget** — home screen (small, medium) and lock screen (circle, rectangle, inline): "128 left", Reviews "120 of 300", New "8 of 20", turning green with a seal once today's minimum is done. Updated whenever the Library loads, and when the app is left part way through a Reviews or New round; after Anki's day rolls over it shows "New day" with an estimate until the app is opened: reviews from the forecast, and as many new cards as today brought.
- **Number on the app icon, turning green** — the red badge on Amgi's icon shows what's left of today's minimum, the widget's number: Reviews + New by default, or Reviews only, or off (Settings → Review → App Icon). It's set whenever the Library loads and when the app is left mid-round. While the app stays closed it moves on by itself at each of the next seven rollovers, to the same estimate as the widget, using badge-only local notifications that show nothing on screen; iOS asks once for the notifications permission (badges only). Once everything it counts is done, the stars turn green: an alternate icon, `AppIconDone` in the asset catalog, listed in `project.yml` under `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_ALTERNATE_APPICON_NAMES` ("Turn green when done" switches it off). iOS changes an app's icon only while it's open and shows an alert each time, so the change waits until nothing else is open, and until the end-of-day summary has been closed; the blue stars come back the first time the app is opened on a new day.
- **Hands-free mode** — for walking or a commute: the headphones button at the top of the review screen (or ⋯ → Hands-Free Mode, or a tap, swipe or shake set to it) reads each card aloud and takes answers by voice. The button is its only sign on screen: green while it runs, pulsing while it listens, orange while the AI voice isn't coming through; a long press shows what it's doing, the last command it took, what the microphone last made out (command or not, to see what it hears when it misses one) and what to say, and if it stops by itself (the microphone or speech recognition turned off, say) an alert says why. Say "show" to turn the card over and hear just the answer (on a cloze, only the revealed cloze; the Extra field is never read), then "again", "hard", "good" or "easy"; or rate straight away and the card is rated without the answer being read. "Repeat", "undo", "bury" (until tomorrow, then on to the next card), "red flag" / "flag red", "orange flag" / "flag orange" ("flag" alone is red; it says which, and listens on without reading the side again) and "stop" work any time, and taps still work. Commands are caught in the recognizer's runner-up guesses too, and in the words it often hears in their place ("heart" for hard, "berry" for bury); Settings → Review → Hands-Free → Sharper listening has Apple's servers hear them instead of the phone, catching more, a moment later, when online. Korean, Japanese and Chinese text gets a voice for that language. Hidden hint content, pictures and the question above the answer line aren't read. You can answer any time, even over the reading: with headphones the microphone doesn't hear it, and out of the speaker or in the car the iPhone's call-style echo cancelling takes it out (the reading is played through the microphone's own audio engine so the canceller knows what to remove), with noise suppression and level keeping for road noise and a quiet voice; a command the side's own text says counts while it's read only when you say it more often than the reading does. Settings → Review → Hands-Free → Talk over the reading turns the echo cancelling off (it makes the reading a little quieter), and then listening waits for the reading to end unless headphones are in. Speech recognition runs on the phone when it can, with a word list of the commands built on the phone the first time (Apple's custom language models), so a lone "good" or "hard" is taken for the command rather than a word that sounds like it. The screen stays on while it runs (Settings → Review → Hands-Free → Keep the screen on), and it keeps going with the screen locked too: background audio, with the microphone kept on for the whole session, since iPhone won't let an app turn it on once the screen is locked. Medical shorthand is read the way it's said: "↑ HR → ↓ CO" as "increased HR leads to decreased CO", "5 mg/kg q6h" as "5 milligrams per kilogram every 6 hours", and likewise ions, units, ranges, blood pressures and the usual abbreviations (Dx, Tx, w/o, s/p…). Reading speed and the iPhone voice are in Settings → Review → Hands-Free, with a sample to hear; by default it's the best voice installed, and a Premium or Enhanced one (a free download in the Settings app: Accessibility → Read & Speak → Voices) sounds far more natural than the basic one.
- **AI voice** — the cards chosen in Settings → Review → AI Voice ("AI voice for": all cards, the default; cards added since the first launch with it; or none) are read in a natural voice from Google Gemini (`gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, with a Gemini key pasted into that screen and kept in the Keychain); any others keep the free iPhone voice. First, unless "Read questions naturally" is off, Gemini (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) rewrites the card the way a tutor would say it: a cloze becomes a spoken question ("What's the drug of choice for absence seizures?") without giving the answer away, shorthand comes out in words, and lists read as speech; a script that names the answer is set aside for the card's own text. Then each side is recorded, compressed to AAC and kept on the phone, out of backups, so it's paid for once: about $2 per 1,000 cards, twice that from January 2027. That happens the first time a card is read hands-free (the next card's while the current one is), or ahead of time with **Prepare Cards**: it finds the cards still to do soonest due first (cards being learned and those due now, then each of the next 60 days' reviews, with new cards worked in at today's new-card count in the order they'll come, then the rest), offers the next 50 or 100 due or all of them with a rough cost, and queues that batch. **Record** sets how: one side at a time (the default), both sides of a card together, or 5, 10 or 20 cards together (up to about 1,200, 2,400 or 4,500 characters of speech in one recording; 20 cards is five minutes or so, and longer recordings are likelier to be cut short, so there's no bigger choice). Google's daily limit counts requests, however much each says, so several sides read in one recording (each a paragraph, with a pause of about two seconds asked for after each, longer than any inside a line) go further: about 100, 400, 800 or 1,600 cards a day against 50, for the same price per second of speech. The recording is cut into a piece per side: the iPhone's own speech recognition, on the phone, hears which words it says and when (helped by the lines' longer words), a stretch of speech at a time, split at its longer pauses, since given a long recording whole it can lose what came before a long pause, each line's words are lined up with what was heard, allowing for words heard a little wrong, and each cut goes in the longest pause between one line's last word and the next one's first. Every piece is then checked: its words must be at least half alike its line's, letter for letter, and more alike than the lines either side, it mustn't be much longer or shorter than the line (a line said twice, or something added), and its length must suit its words. A piece that passes is kept, with a moment's quiet either side and the ends faded, like a recording of its own. The lines that don't pass are recorded together once more, one request for all of them, and any that still don't, and every line when speech recognition isn't allowed, are recorded on their own, so no card keeps a bad cut. The test shows why a piece didn't pass: none of its words heard, a line next to it not heard, no pause between it and the next line, words that don't match, and so on; Activity notes how many words were heard in how many stretches. Hands-free mode, making a card ready as it goes, still records a side at a time. **Prepare new cards by itself** (on unless switched off) does it without Prepare Cards: the first time, every card still without the AI voice is queued, soonest due first, and after that the cards added since it last looked (by card id, the moment a card was added), whenever Amgi opens; the queue is then worked through within Google's daily limit. It carries on out of sight too: on iOS 26, work started while Amgi is open carries on once it's left, as a continued-processing background task whose progress iOS shows (a card's share as each is done, and a little every few seconds while Gemini records, since iOS ends work it sees standing still); and whenever there's work, Amgi asks iOS for a processing task, which it usually runs overnight with the iPhone charging and online, to find new cards and work through the queue until iOS wants the time back. In the background, before a recording of several cards is asked for, speech recognition is tried once on a recording made already: if it doesn't run there, nothing is spent and the cards wait for Amgi to be open. **Test Recording Together** tries it on ten cards due soon (ones already recorded a side at a time first): five cards in one recording and five with both sides in one recording each, six recordings in all, kept apart from the AI voice's own. It shows how many sides passed, why any didn't, and how long Gemini took, with each piece to play beside the recording made on its own, and the Record choice underneath. The queue is worked through one card at a time with the screen kept on, showing progress, what it's doing that moment ("Now: Recording the answer of …") and a Stop button; it's kept between launches, carries on by itself whenever Amgi is open, waits while hands-free runs, and can be carried on or cleared. While it waits, a Status row says why and when it carries on, in local time. Google lets the Gemini voice make about 100 recordings a day once billing is on for the key, roughly 50 cards, only about 10 on its free tier, and about 10 a minute: when Google asks for a pause the AI voice waits out the minute, and when the day's limit is reached it rests until midnight in California (remembered across launches), the queue waits for the next day, and hands-free reads in the iPhone voice meanwhile. When Google's refusal names the limit, or the free tier, the message says so and how to raise it; Try Again Now (or queuing a new batch) makes one more attempt straight away, after billing is turned on, say, and the queue tries again by itself an hour after Google said no whenever Amgi is open, so a raised limit is picked up without waiting for the next day. Settings shows how many of the cards the AI voice reads have it ("52 of 903 (5%)") and how many are still to do, counted in the background when the screen opens (suspended cards, and cards with nothing to read, aren't counted) and kept up as cards are made ready, by the queue or hands-free, with roughly how many days the rest take once Google has given the key's daily limit. It also counts the recordings made today, by Google's day ("7 of 10" once Google has given the key's limit), and **Activity** lists what the AI voice has done lately, newest first: each card's script and recordings, each card made ready, and every refusal in Google's own words, kept between launches (the last 300), with Copy All and Clear. A recording that isn't ready within a few seconds also leaves that card to the iPhone voice, reading the script if there is one; the hands-free button turns orange, and a long press on it says why. Settings also has the voice (Gemini's 30, Sulafat by default), a sample to hear, and a button that deletes the scripts and recordings. The key screen says which kind of key is saved (Gemini takes only auth keys, starting "AQ.", since September 2026, so an older "AIza" key is flagged for replacing) and has a Test Key button that rewrites a sample card; when Google refuses a key, the message says what to do before Google's own words.
- **Explain** — once the answer is showing, an Explain button above the rating bar (also in ⋯ and as a tap/swipe/shake action) asks OpenAI why the answer is right, with the key context, using the Graveyard's key and model. Ask follow-up questions in the same sheet.
- **Every due card before repeats** — on by default when studying a deck: a card you miss, or one still in learning, waits until every other due card has been shown once. Only the order changes; the engine still schedules each answer when you give it. Settings → Review → Card Order switches back to Anki's usual order.
- **Taps, swipes and shake** — nine tap areas on the card, four swipes, and a shake of the phone, each set to show the answer, rate, undo, replay audio, flag, edit the note, or capture a visual mnemonic (Settings → Review Behavior → Taps, Swipes & Shake). A rating on the question side shows the answer instead; taps on links, audio buttons, and looked-up words behave as before, and a swipe that scrolls a long card counts as scrolling.
- **Keyboard control** — rate, reveal, and undo from a hardware keyboard, with the typed-answer field staying up across the reveal.
- **Dual card rendering** — the Rust template engine renders cards exactly like desktop clients. Simple cards are auto-detected and drawn by a native SwiftUI renderer; complex cards fall back to a sandboxed WebKit host. ⋯ → Card Rendering shows which path the card is on ("HTML · auto · Cloze") and pins the render mode, globally or per template; it used to be a bar above every card. A card starts near the top of the screen, leaving the room below for what the answer adds (Settings → Review → Content alignment → Center puts it in the middle instead). Turning a card over keeps its text where it was: the answer starts where the question sat on screen (rather than being centred again, which moved the text up by half of whatever the answer adds), what it adds runs on below, and the page scrolls if it has to; it scrolls by itself only when the answer line would be below the screen. A note's Extra field (the first field with "extra" in its name) gets a blank line's gap above it on the answer side when the template leaves none: the app marks where the field's HTML begins in the rendered answer, and the card page measures the gap there.
- **MathJax and audio** — formula rendering and card audio playback in both renderers.
- **Today view** — an aggregate "due now" screen across every deck, with an up-next queue ordered by workload.
- **Graveyard** — a tab for every card flagged red or orange, however overdue, kept out of the Reviews button until it's dealt with. For each: ask an AI (OpenAI, same key as the pictures) whether it's right and have it propose a fix you can apply in one tap, add a diagram or mnemonic picture to its Extra field, edit it by hand, mark it fixed, or delete it. Marking it fixed starts the card over as a new card, so the fixed version is learned from scratch: Anki's Reset with its original place among the new cards restored where Anki knows it, and its review and lapse counts cleared so the problem-card rule doesn't flag it straight back; then the flag is cleared. Settings moved from the tab bar to the Library's More menu to make room. Cards you keep forgetting arrive on their own: once a card has been forgotten 5 times (Settings → Review → Graveyard: off, or 3 to 10), missing it again flags it orange as the session closes, and a button there flags the cards already past that.
- **Visual mnemonics** (experimental) — tap ✨ while reviewing to save an idea for a picture onto the card, then carry on. Later, from Library → Mnemonics, edit each description, generate a draft, and approve it into the card's Extra field (or Back Extra / Back) or discard it. Nothing reaches the card until you approve. Ideas are stored inside the note, so they sync like any other edit. Pictures come from OpenAI's image API once you save an API key (Mnemonics → ⚙︎); until then drafts are free placeholder squares. The key is kept in the Keychain, drafts are kept on the device until approved, and pictures are stored as 768 px JPEGs.

## Decks

- **Hierarchical deck tree** — recursive expand/collapse with new/learning/review count badges on every node, as a list or a grid.
- **Deck detail** — per-deck counts, retention, average cards/day, mature card count, and subdeck breakdown.
- **Per-deck study options** — FSRS weights editor with optimizer and simulator, preset CRUD, Easy Days, bury rules, review timer, auto-advance.
- **Custom study and filtered decks** — extend today's new-card and review limits per deck, and rebuild or empty a filtered deck to return its cards to their home decks.
- **Filtered deck presets** — save named searches (query, card limit, gather order, reschedule flag) and build any selection of them into filtered decks in one tap. Presets are app-local and per profile; the decks they build are ordinary Anki filtered decks and sync like any other.
- **Import** — `.apkg` and `.colpkg` import, including from the iOS share sheet, with progress shown while it runs. Text files with one note per line (`.tsv`, `.csv`, `.txt`) go through Anki's own CSV importer: a sheet shows the first lines and lets you choose the separator (a `.tsv` starts on Tab), whether fields hold HTML, the note type, the deck, which column fills each field, a tags column, and what happens to notes already there (update, leave, or add again); file headers like `#separator:` and `#deck:` are honoured. Library ⋯ → Import Deck or Text File….

## Notes

- **Browser** — search the whole collection with Anki search syntax, deck filter chips (a top-level deck includes its subdecks), tag chips, sorting, and lazy-loaded results.
- **Editor** — rich field editing with accurate field names pulled from the Rust notetype RPC; multi-select for batch operations. Paste (top left) adds whatever you've copied, text or pictures, to the end of the Extra field (or Back Extra / Back / the last field), with no "Allow Paste?" prompt. A picture can also be pasted straight into any field while typing (Paste in the menu, or ⌘V), when adding a note, editing one, or in an image occlusion's Header and Back Extra: it goes where the cursor is, and a field that was plain text is edited as HTML from then on. A field edited as HTML has a small "HTML" tag by its name, in place of a line of explanation under it. Pictures show in the fields as themselves (up to 240 × 180 points), not as their `<img>` tags, which are written back exactly as they were; one whose file isn't in the media folder shows as its tag. Bold, italic and cloze changes only add or remove tags around the words, so a picture among them stays, and Copy or Cut of a selection with a picture keeps it, to paste as the picture again. Pictures are stored as media named for their content, as Anki names pastes, at most 1600 px and as JPEGs unless they have see-through parts.
- **Cloze cards** — Add Note starts on the Cloze note type (Anki's own when it's there, otherwise the first cloze type, never Image Occlusion), unless what's being added asks for another. In the field cloze deletions go in (the one the template reads with `{{cloze:…}}`), when adding or editing, the bar above the keyboard starts with the cloze tools: **Cloze** hides the selection on a card of its own (the next number, `{{c3::…}}`; with nothing selected it makes an empty one to type into), **Same Card** hides it on the card of the highest number used, together with what's there, the **c1/c2** button shows the card the deletion at the cursor is on and moves it to any other, or to a new one, **Hint** adds a hint (or selects the one there), **Unwrap** takes the deletion away and keeps its words, and **Renumber** numbers them all in order from c1 with no gaps. Spaces at the ends of a selection stay outside the deletion, each change can be undone, and Done stays in reach at the end of the bar. Under the field a line says which cards the note makes ("Makes 3 cards: c1, c2, c3"), or how to make the first; a cloze note with nothing hidden isn't added, with a note saying what to do.
- **Adding notes one after another** — the note type and deck are two small menus in Add Note's top bar (the deck by the last part of its name; filtered decks, which can't take new notes, aren't offered), not sections of the form, and the deck starts as the one the last note went in, unless Add Note was opened from a deck. Tags are a single row, an error comes up as an alert where the keyboard can't hide it, and Image Occlusion's deck is a menu in its top bar too. Add Note stays open after each note, as Anki's Add window does: an "Added" toast (with how many so far) and a haptic, the fields start empty for the next note, the cursor goes to the first of them, and the deck, note type and tags stay. Tap the pin by a field's name to keep what's in it from note to note (a source, a picture, a passage you're making several cards from); pins are remembered for each note type on the phone and start from the fields pinned ("sticky") in Anki. Add needs something in a field that isn't pinned; Cancel becomes Done once a note is added, and closes without asking when nothing new has been typed. Adding a word from the dictionary lookup still closes after the one note.
- **Tags** — batch tagging from a selection, plus collection-wide tag management.
- **Duplicates** — find notes that share a first field, which is the question Anki's `dupe:` operator does not answer.
- **Image occlusion** — create and edit occlusion notes with rectangle, ellipse, polygon, and text masks, with reviewer parity with upstream Anki.
- **Card templates** — a full template editor with front/back/styling source editing, live preview, validation, and notetype field management.

## Reader

- **EPUB reader** — read books chapter by chapter with native paging, vertical writing mode, and Latin/CJK auto-detection.
- **Offline dictionary lookup** — tap any word in the book reader for a Yomitan-compatible lookup, with chained popups, search history, and per-dictionary collapsed memory. It isn't on the review screen: a tap on a card is for the tap areas (reveal, rate and so on), and a word lookup there kept catching those taps.
- **Text-to-speech** — a speak button with language-aware voice selection.
- **Bundled Korean fonts** — Sarasa Mono K, Nanum Myeongjo, Nanum Gothic.
- **Progress sync** — reading position travels with the collection across devices.

## Sync and accounts

- **Any compatible sync server** — AnkiWeb or self-hosted: login, incremental sync, full upload/download, media sync, and bidirectional review sync.
- **Safe sync merge** — when local and server collections diverge, merge them (keeping cards from both sides) instead of being forced to overwrite one. Destructive choices require explicit confirmation.
- **Multi-profile accounts** — isolated collections per profile, a fast picker in the Library toolbar, and per-profile sync credentials and review history.
- **Offline-first** — everything works offline; sync when you have a connection.

## Statistics

- **Dashboard** — all-time and windowed summaries (reviewed, time, young, mature, new, relearn), future-due forecast with backlog toggle, stacked review history, and card count breakdown.
- **Review heatmap** — full-year contribution-style heatmap that auto-scrolls to today, with a streak counter.
- **Streak card** — current streak against the same point last month, at the top of the dashboard.
- **VoiceOver** — every chart is readable as an audio summary, not an unlabelled image.
- **Scoping** — every graph can be scoped to the whole collection or one deck, over Today / 7 days / 1 month / 3 months / 1 year / all time.

## Beyond the phone

- **Home-screen widgets** — small, medium, and large families driven by a precomputed forecast timeline, configurable per deck through AppIntents, with no background refresh required.
- **Apple Watch app** — deck list, card review with audio playback, stats, and sync, with the Anki engine running on-device.
- **Shortcuts and Siri** — study a deck, sync the collection, or ask how many cards are due, from Shortcuts, Spotlight, or by voice.

## Maintenance and appearance

- **Guided first run** — a four-step welcome tour before the sync choice, skippable at any point.
- **Multi-theme system** — Vivid and Muted palettes with Light/Dark/Follow-System, shared with the widgets through an App Group. Every bundled palette meets WCAG AA contrast, and a test keeps it that way.
- **Database tools** — check database, find empty cards, media check, and backup management.

## Under the hood

- **Swift 6.2 strict concurrency** — language mode v6, fully actor-isolated, `Sendable` throughout.
- **Rust owns the data** — Swift never touches SQLite. Every read and write goes through the engine's RPC seam.
